Texas Voter Guide

A guide to Texas ballots in which every candidate, endorsement, and donation is tied to a public record you can open and read yourself.

Tarrant County is the only county covered so far. It is the one county with a ballot loaded today, and more Texas counties are being added. Until then, nothing on this page describes a ballot outside Tarrant County.

Find the races on your ballot

Enter your street address and this page will work out which of the 707 Tarrant County voting precincts you live in, then show only the races you are actually eligible to vote in. This lookup covers Tarrant County only — an address anywhere else in Texas will not match a precinct yet.

Your address stays in your browser. It is sent to exactly one place — the U.S. Census Bureau’s free public geocoder at geocoding.geo.census.gov — which turns it into a map coordinate and returns it. It is never sent to this site, never stored, and never logged: this site is static files with no server and no database. Matching that coordinate to your precinct happens entirely on your own device.

Your ballot is not the whole county

No races are listed yet, on purpose. Tarrant County has 85 certified races for November 3, and no one votes in all of them — congressional, state legislative, State Board of Education, county commissioner, and justice of the peace seats are drawn by district. Enter your street address above and this page lists the races you are eligible to vote in, and nothing else.

Opens the full county list — all 85 races and 153 candidates, with filters, search, and printing. It is not a ballot: yours is a subset of it.

Key dates

  • Oct 19–30 Early voting Monday, October 19 through Friday, October 30. Any early voting site in Tarrant County will take your ballot, not just the one near your home.
  • Nov 3 Election day Tuesday, November 3. Polls are open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.

The last day to register is 30 days before the election. For November 3 that is October 4, which falls on a Sunday, so registrations are accepted through the next business day — Monday, October 5. Check your registration status at votetexas.gov.

Candidates are real — endorsements and donations are not loaded yet

Every race name, candidate name, and party label below is transcribed from the Texas Secretary of State’s Ballot Certification Report for the November 3, 2026 general election in Tarrant County. The endorsement and donation sections are empty for every candidate: that data has not been collected yet.

Where this data comes from

153 candidates across 85 races
Taken from the Texas Secretary of State’s certified ballot report for the November 3, 2026 general election in Tarrant County, dated September 7, 2026. Race names, candidate names, and party labels are as printed in that report. Open the PDF — it is checked into this site at sources/ballot-certification-2026-11-03.pdf.
707 voting precincts
Precinct boundaries, and the congressional, legislative, SBOE, commissioner, and JP districts attached to each one, come from Tarrant County’s public GIS. The address lookup tests your coordinate against these polygons in your own browser. Voting Precincts, Tarrant County Open Data Portal — the copy this page reads is data/precincts.geojson.

What this covers — and what it doesn’t

  • Every race on the Tarrant County ballot. All 85 certified races, from U.S. Senate down to justice of the peace, with every candidate and party as certified.
  • No endorsement or campaign finance data yet. Those sections are empty for every candidate. Nothing is added until it can carry a link to the record it came from.
  • One county so far. Tarrant is the only ballot loaded. An address anywhere else in Texas will not match a precinct.

How we source this

Every claim in this guide links to a public record. Endorsements point to the endorsing organization’s own announcement or to published news coverage. Donations point to the campaign finance report they were filed in. Nothing is included unless a link can be attached to it, and nothing here is inferred, summarized, or ranked — if a line looks wrong, open its source and judge it yourself.
